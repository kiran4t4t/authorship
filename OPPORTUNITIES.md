**Compiled:** 2026-09-18 · **Sources:** live web search (publisher sites were unreachable from this
environment, so every date below is from search-result text, not from the CFP page itself).

> **Verify every deadline on the linked page before you write anything.** Deadlines get extended,
> moved, and silently corrected. Items marked **UNVERIFIED** or **CONFLICTING** below are ones where
> the sources disagreed or the official page did not surface a date.

> **Legal note:** whether any of these venues satisfies USCIS's "authorship of scholarly articles in
> professional or major trade publications or other major media" (8 CFR 204.5(h)(3)(vi)) or the O-1
> equivalent is a legal determination. Take the shortlist to your immigration attorney before you
> build a filing strategy around any single venue.

## 2. Rolling submissions — no deadline, submit any time

These are the ones to use when you want to start *this month* rather than wait for a window.

- **IEEE Software** — regular submissions plus special-issue proposals. Explicitly welcomes articles on
  how software is built inside specific companies, new tools, current trends, and candid accounts of
  project limitations and failures. (Authorship)
- **IEEE IT Professional** — bimonthly IEEE Computer Society publication aimed at developers and
  managers of enterprise information systems. Original, state-of-the-art submissions, rolling. (Authorship)
- **IEEE Internet Computing** — rolling submissions between themed issues. Its
  "Future Internet Systems with LLMs and Agents" special issue closed 15 Sep 2026 (three days before
  this sweep) — the regular track is still open. (Authorship)
- **TMLR (Transactions on Machine Learning Research)** — rolling submission, no deadlines, and a
  standing reviewer self-nomination form. (Authorship + Judging)

paper trail is the evidence, not the fact of being listed.

## 5. Avoid these

Several venues surfaced during this sweep that show predatory or paper-mill patterns — solicitation-driven
CFPs, near-instant acceptance, fee-first models, no real editorial board. Publishing in them is worse
than not publishing: it is a visible negative signal to an adjudicator reviewing your record.

- `futureaiconferences.com` ("AICon 2027")
- IJIRMPS (`ijirmps.org`)
- IAEME Publication (`iaeme.com`), incl. IJRCAIT
- `espjeta.org` conference proceedings

Before submitting anywhere not on the Tier A/B list, check: is the publisher IEEE, ACM, USENIX, Springer,
Elsevier, Taylor & Francis, or a named university press? Is there a real, named editorial board with
institutional affiliations? Is there a documented peer-review process with realistic timelines?

## 6. Suggested sequencing

1. **Now → 1 Nov 2026:** IEEE SSCI 2027 late-breaking poster abstract (250 words). Lowest cost, real
   IEEE venue, gets a first item on the board and a conference presentation for the Speaking criterion.
2. **Now → Nov/Dec 2026:** IEEE Computer AI-Native SE special issue as an experience report — *after*
   confirming whether the deadline is 1 Nov or 1 Dec.
3. **Oct 2026:** Watch AAAI-27 workshop CFPs as they post; pick one and submit, and email the organizers
   volunteering to review.
4. **Oct 2026 → Mar 2027:** MIS Quarterly Executive agentic-AI paper. Longest runway, best fit, most
   durable evidence. Consider the ICIS Lisbon paper development workshop in December.
5. **Dec 2026 → Mar 2027:** VLDB 2027 Industrial Track, if you have a system with real production
   numbers behind it.
6. **Ongoing:** TMLR reviewer self-nomination; IEEE Software / IT Professional rolling submissions.
7. **Jun–Sep 2027:** IAAI-28 Deployed Application paper. Begin collecting deployment metrics now —
   that track requires measurable benefit from a system already in production with end users.
